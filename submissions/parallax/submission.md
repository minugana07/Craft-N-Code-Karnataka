## Project summary

Parallax is an AI-powered teacher intelligence platform that helps educators connect assessment evidence to learning gaps, plan targeted interventions, and track follow-up outcomes. It supports teacher decision-making rather than replacing teachers, helping educators look beyond scores to understand where students may need support.

## Short answers

### 1. What made you choose this problem?

In higher education, assessment often ends when marks are published. A score can indicate how a student performed, but it may not clearly explain which concepts need reinforcement, whether a difficulty is recurring across assessments, or whether a teaching intervention has helped. Teachers managing multiple classes and large student groups also face the challenge of connecting assessment results, learning gaps, and follow-up actions over time.

We chose this problem because we believe educational data should lead to meaningful action rather than remain confined to marksheets and dashboards. Parallax addresses this gap through a Teacher Memory Engine that connects assessment evidence, potential learning gaps, teacher-approved interventions, and subsequent follow-up outcomes. Instead of treating every assessment as an isolated event, the platform is designed to help educators build a more continuous understanding of student learning.

Our central idea is that AI should strengthen a teacher’s ability to make informed decisions, not replace professional judgment. Parallax therefore focuses on evidence-linked insights, teacher-controlled interventions, and longitudinal context. The goal is to help educators look beyond the score, identify where support may be needed, and make the next teaching decision more purposeful.

### 2. Who is it for, and how would they use it in a normal week?

Parallax is designed primarily for college faculty members who teach multiple classes, conduct regular assessments, and need to monitor learning progress across a semester. It is particularly relevant in environments where educators handle large student groups and must balance teaching, assessment, feedback, and remediation.

A typical weekly workflow begins with reviewing class and assessment performance. Following a quiz, assignment, or test, the teacher can examine performance patterns and identify concepts or learning areas that may require further attention. Parallax is designed to connect this evidence to potential learning gaps, helping the teacher investigate recurring difficulties rather than relying only on aggregate marks.

The teacher can then plan and record a targeted intervention, such as revisiting a concept, conducting a revision session, or providing additional practice. In a subsequent assessment or follow-up, the teacher can review the available evidence to understand whether the intended learning outcome appears to have improved and decide what to do next.

The key differentiator is continuity: assessment evidence informs the identification of learning gaps, those gaps inform teacher-approved interventions, and follow-up evidence informs subsequent decisions. Rather than making unsupported judgments about students or automatically prescribing teaching actions, Parallax aims to provide educators with a structured, evolving view of the learning process. This can make the platform useful not just after examinations, but throughout the semester.

### 3. If you had another month, what would you build next?

With another month, our priority would be to strengthen Parallax across three dimensions: evidence quality, personalised teaching support, and real-world validation.

First, we would develop a more robust AI-assisted learning-gap analysis system. Instead of presenting a potential gap without context, the platform should connect each insight to relevant assessment evidence, indicate uncertainty where appropriate, and distinguish observed patterns from AI-generated interpretations. We would also evaluate how well these insights align with faculty judgments before expanding their use.

Second, we would deepen the Teacher Memory Engine by improving the connection between learning gaps, interventions, and follow-up outcomes. We would explore intervention recommendations that teachers can review and modify, better tracking of whether planned actions were completed, and longitudinal views that help educators understand how learning patterns evolve across assessments. We would also investigate integration with existing learning-management and assessment systems to reduce repetitive data entry.

Third, we would validate the platform with faculty members using appropriately consented and protected educational data. We would evaluate the accuracy and usefulness of identified learning gaps, the relevance of suggested interventions, usability for teachers managing multiple classes, and the quality of follow-up insights. We would also strengthen access controls, data privacy, auditability, testing, and deployment reliability.

Our objective would not simply be to add more AI features. It would be to establish whether Parallax genuinely helps teachers move from assessment evidence to better-informed teaching decisions. The long-term vision is a system that remembers the educational context behind the marks while keeping teachers accountable for decisions and students free from permanent, reductive labels.
